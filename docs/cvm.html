Overview Experimental (0.18)

The cvm family models structural equation models that contain both latent variables (common factors) and composites. It implements the factor- and composite-based SEM framework of Schamberger, Schuberth, Henseler and Rosseel (2026), and extends it in the way psychonetrics extends other model families: every variance–covariance structure in the model can also be modeled as a network.

  • cvm(): the general model, with factor loadings (lambda), composite weights (weights), regressions between the constructs (beta) and three covariance structures that can each be parameterized in several ways.
  • cnm(): the composite network model, a wrapper that models the indicators within every composite as a partial correlation network, and by default the constructs as well (latent = "ggm").
Experimental: this family is new in psychonetrics 0.18 and marked experimental — a note is shown once per session. Arguments, defaults and output may still change. Please report unexpected behavior on GitHub.

Latent Variables and Composites

A latent variable is a common cause of its indicators and is measured with error. A composite is the opposite: an exact weighted sum of its indicators, without any error. Both are called constructs below. Every observed variable is either an indicator of latent variables ($\boldsymbol{y}_f$) or an indicator of exactly one composite ($\boldsymbol{y}_c$):

$$\boldsymbol{y}_f = \boldsymbol{\nu}_f + \boldsymbol{\Lambda}\,\boldsymbol{\eta}_f + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\eta}_c = \boldsymbol{W}^{\top} \boldsymbol{y}_c, \qquad \boldsymbol{\eta} = \boldsymbol{\nu}_\eta + \boldsymbol{B}\,\boldsymbol{\eta} + \boldsymbol{\zeta}$$

  • $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ (lambda): the factor loadings of the latent variables.
  • $\boldsymbol{W}$ (weights): the weights with which indicators form their composite.
  • $\boldsymbol{B}$ (beta): the regressions between constructs, which can be latent variables or composites in any combination.

The composite model does not explain why the indicators of a composite are correlated: these variances and covariances are free parameters. What it does impose is that the indicators of a composite relate to everything else in the model only through the composite. That restriction is what makes the model testable.

Observed variables in the structural model: a composite with a single indicator is that observed variable itself. This is the way to use an observed variable as a predictor or outcome, as in the first example below. A latent variable with a single indicator (one loading in lambda) gives the same model. A variable cannot load directly on a composite: effects between constructs are always specified in beta.

Model Matrices and the _c Suffix

The family has three covariance structures, each with its own type argument:

ArgumentWhat it modelsSuffixTypes
latent (Residual) variances and covariances of the constructs _zeta "cov", "ggm"
residual Residuals of the indicators of latent variables _epsilon "cov", "chol", "prec", "ggm", "cor"
composite Variances and covariances of the indicators of composites _c "cov", "chol", "prec", "ggm", "cor"

The matrices with the suffix _c (sigma_c, omega_c, delta_c, kappa_c, lowertri_c, rho_c, SD_c) describe the indicators of composites. They are block diagonal with one block per composite: elements within a block are parameters, whereas elements that connect indicators of different composites are always zero, because those covariances are implied by the relations between the composites.

Note: the suffix _c does not refer to relations between composites. Those are modeled, like relations between latent variables, in beta and the _zeta matrices.

Some quantities are computed rather than estimated. The variance of a composite follows from its weights and the covariances of its indicators, so the (residual) variances and the intercepts of composites are not parameters. They are shown in the parameter table without standard errors. getmatrix() also returns a few derived matrices: the composite loadings (in the columns of lambda that belong to composites), the implied covariances and means of the constructs (sigma_eta, mu_eta), and the implied residual covariances of the composite indicators (sigma_epsilon_c).

Example: Composites Predicting an Observed Variable

The StarWars data (271 respondents) contain three questions on the original trilogy (Q5–Q7), three on the sequel trilogy (Q8–Q10), and the item “I am a huge Star Wars fan!” (Q1). We form one composite per trilogy and use both to predict being a fan: 

library("psychonetrics")
library("dplyr")
data(StarWars)

vars <- c("Q1", paste0("Q", 5:10))

# Weights: which variables form which composite. Q1 is a composite with a
# single indicator, which represents the observed variable itself:
weights <- matrix(0, 7, 3)
weights[1, 1]   <- 1   # Fan
weights[2:4, 2] <- 1   # Originals
weights[5:7, 3] <- 1   # Sequels

# Both composites predict being a fan (they are correlated by default):
beta <- matrix(0, 3, 3)
beta[1, 2:3] <- 1

mod <- cvm(StarWars, weights = weights, beta = beta, vars = vars,
           latents = c("Fan", "Originals", "Sequels")) %>% runmodel

mod %>% fit          # chi-square = 2.92, df = 8, p = 0.94
mod %>% parameters

getmatrix(mod, "weights")   # estimated weights
getmatrix(mod, "lambda")    # composite loadings

The model fits well. The weights and effects are:

ParameterEstimateSE
Q5 → Originals0.370.12
Q6 → Originals1 (fixed)
Q7 → Originals0.300.12
Q8 → Sequels1 (fixed)
Q9 → Sequels−0.230.22
Q10 → Sequels1.230.38
Originals → Fan0.470.061
Sequels → Fan0.130.040

One weight of every composite is fixed to 1 to set its scale, so the other weights are relative to that indicator. By default this is the indicator with the largest standardized starting weight (here Q6 and Q8; a message reports that Originals is not scaled by its first indicator). Weights are estimated from the relations of a composite with the rest of the model, and therefore tend to have larger standard errors than factor loadings.

The Composite Network Model: cnm()

With composite = "ggm", or the wrapper cnm(), the indicators within each composite are modeled as a Gaussian graphical model: omega_c contains one partial correlation network per composite. cnm() by default also models the constructs as a network (latent = "ggm", omega_zeta); set latent = "cov" to model their covariances instead. With all edges included this is the same model as above in another parameterization. The networks can then be made sparse with the usual tools: 

mod_cnm <- cnm(StarWars, weights = weights, beta = beta, vars = vars,
               latents = c("Fan", "Originals", "Sequels")) %>% runmodel

# Remove non-significant edges within the composites:
mod_cnm_pruned <- mod_cnm %>% prune(alpha = 0.05)

compare(full = mod_cnm, pruned = mod_cnm_pruned)
#   model DF     AIC     BIC RMSEA Chisq Chisq_diff DF_diff p_value
#    full  8 5799.02 5896.27   ~ 0  2.92
#  pruned 10 5800.94 5890.99   ~ 0  8.84       5.92       2   0.052

# One network per composite:
getmatrix(mod_cnm_pruned, "omega_c")

Pruning removes the two edges that involve Q9, leaving a fully connected network of the three questions on the original trilogy (partial correlations 0.26, 0.16 and 0.38) and a single edge between Q8 and Q10 (0.37) for the sequels. prune(), stepup() and modelsearch() never add edges between indicators of different composites.

Because the unpruned cnm model is the cvm model in another parameterization, a fitted model can also be transformed from one to the other with transmod(), without estimating it again: 

# cvm -> cnm (covariances of the composite indicators -> networks):
mod_cnm2 <- transmod(mod, composite = "ggm", keep_computed = TRUE)

# cnm -> cvm:
mod_cvm2 <- transmod(mod_cnm, composite = "cov", keep_computed = TRUE)

This works when every composite is saturated (all covariances or all edges among its indicators), and also for the other types ("chol", "prec", "cor"), the residual type and the latent type ("cov" or "ggm").

Combining a Latent Variable and a Composite

Latent variables and composites can be combined freely. Here the sequel questions are modeled as indicators of a latent variable, predicted by a composite of the questions on the original trilogy: 

vars2 <- paste0("Q", 5:10)

lambda2 <- matrix(0, 6, 2)
lambda2[4:6, 2] <- 1      # Sequels: a latent variable

weights2 <- matrix(0, 6, 2)
weights2[1:3, 1] <- 1     # Originals: a composite

beta2 <- matrix(0, 2, 2)
beta2[2, 1] <- 1          # Originals -> Sequels

mod2 <- cvm(StarWars, lambda = lambda2, weights = weights2, beta = beta2,
            vars = vars2, latents = c("Originals", "Sequels")) %>% runmodel

mod2 %>% fit          # chi-square = 3.01, df = 6, p = 0.81
mod2 %>% parameters   # Sequels <- Originals: 0.17 (SE 0.059)

A construct is a latent variable when it has elements in lambda and a composite when it has elements in weights; it cannot have both, and a variable cannot be an indicator of a composite and load on a latent variable at the same time.

A Network of Constructs

With latent = "ggm" the relations between the constructs are modeled as a partial correlation network (omega_zeta) instead of covariances, as in the latent network model of the lvm family: 

mod3 <- cvm(StarWars, weights = weights, vars = vars, latent = "ggm",
            latents = c("Fan", "Originals", "Sequels")) %>% runmodel

getmatrix(mod3, "omega_zeta")

The estimated partial correlations are 0.525 between being a fan and the composite of the original trilogy, 0.228 between being a fan and the composite of the sequels, and 0.259 between the two composites. With all three edges included, this model is equivalent to the first example (chi-square = 2.92, df = 8).

One restriction: with latent = "ggm", a composite can either be a node in the latent network or have incoming effects in beta, but not both. Such models are refused with an informative error, and the model search functions do not propose them. Which of the two a composite is follows from the model as you specify it; after adding or removing effects on composites by hand, identify() updates which parameters model search may add.

Searching for Clusters: clustersearch() Experimental (0.18)

When it is not known in advance which variables form a composite, clustersearch() searches for clusters of variables. The search starts with the saturated model, in which all variables are freely related. In every step it combines the two clusters (or variables that are not yet clustered) of which the combination gives the lowest BIC, until two clusters are left. Every cluster is a composite of its variables, and all clusters and remaining variables are freely related. The model with the lowest BIC over all steps is selected: 

data(NA2020)

res <- clustersearch(NA2020)
res          # the steps of the search and the selected model
plot(res)    # dendrogram with the BIC on the same axis

res$clusters         # the variables of every cluster
res$model            # the selected model, a regular cvm model
res$models[["4"]]    # the model of another step, here with four clusters

For the eight items on sleep and well-being in the NA2020 data, the selected model has three composites: worrying about sleep with sleep interfering with daily functioning; a regular sleep schedule, being happy with one's health and feeling alone; and being optimistic, being happy and being happy with one's love life (BIC = 14999.90, against 15058.33 for the saturated model).

  • cluster = "latent" searches for latent variables instead of composites, using the lvm family. All clusters in a search are of the same kind. For a cluster of two variables the two give the same fit; they differ from three variables on.
  • type = "ggm" returns network models: cnm models for composites and latent network models for latent variables.
  • criterion sets the fit measure that is minimized (for example "aic" or "ebic.5"), and nCores estimates the candidate models of a step in parallel.
  • Arguments on the data and the estimator (such as groupvar, covs and nobs, or sumscores = TRUE for sum scores) are passed on to cvm() or lvm().
Keep in mind: the search is greedy. A combination is never undone, and a cluster of three or more variables can only be found by first combining two of its variables. The number of candidate models grows quickly with the number of variables (28 in the first step for 8 variables, 190 for 20), so the search can take long for many variables.

Scaling and Identification

  • Scaling. A composite is scaled by fixing the weight of one indicator to 1: by default the indicator with the largest standardized starting weight (bad_marker_crit = 1). A message names the composites for which that is not the first indicator. Scaling by an indicator with a small weight makes the other weights large and can reverse the composite, in which case the optimizer can stop before the optimum; a warning is given when a composite correlates negatively with its scaling indicator. Set bad_marker_crit = 0 to always use the first indicator, or a value between 0 and 1 to only replace a first indicator whose starting weight is below that proportion of the largest.
  • Choosing the scaling yourself. Free the fixed weight with freepar() and fix another with fixpar(). The scaling does not change the fit of the model, but it does change the weights and the effects of and on the composite.
  • Sum scores. With sumscores = TRUE all weights are fixed to 1, so that every composite is the sum score of its indicators and the structural model is a model for the sum scores. This model is nested in the model with estimated weights, so the two can be compared with compare(). Modification indices are computed for the fixed weights, and single weights can be freed with freepar(). Use fixpar() to fix the weights of only some composites.
  • Dependent indicators. The indicators of a composite may not be linearly dependent (for example a total score next to its items): the weights are then not identified, and cvm() stops with an error. When the indicators are nearly dependent (a condition number of their correlation matrix above 1000) a warning is given, as the weights are then poorly determined.
  • Isolated composites. A composite with estimated weights must be related to at least one other construct. The weights of a composite that is not related to anything are not identified, and a warning is given. prune() can remove the last relation of a composite with the other constructs; it then gives this warning, and the weights of that composite should be fixed with fixpar().
  • Latent variables are identified as in the lvm family (identification = "loadings" or "variance").

Estimation

The estimators "ML", "FIML" (chosen automatically with missing data), "ULS", "WLS" and "DWLS" are available, for single and multiple groups with equality constraints (equal, groupequal()) and with a mean structure. The model is implemented in both the R and the C++ pipeline, which give identical results.

Estimation starts from partial least squares estimates of the weights and uses the same optimizer and settings as all other model families. When all constructs are composites that are all related to each other (and there are no effects in beta), the maximum likelihood weights minimize the determinant of the correlation matrix of the composites, and the starting weights are improved on that criterion, so that such a model starts at or close to its optimum. Composite weights are only weakly identified, which makes the likelihood flat in some directions: the optimizer can stop slightly before the optimum. Stricter convergence criteria can be set with setoptimizer(), for example setoptimizer(mod, "nlminb", optim.args = list(control = list(rel.tol = 1e-10, eval.max = 20000, iter.max = 10000))). A warning is given when the optimizer does not report convergence, when the indicator that scales a composite has a small weight relative to the other indicators or correlates negatively with the composite, when a composite with estimated weights is not related to any other construct, or when the computed residual variance of a composite is not positive.

Penalized estimation: estimator = "PML" penalizes the network structures in the model, after which refit() gives unpenalized estimates of the selected network. This works, but the optimizer used for penalized estimation is a first-order method and can be very slow for composite models (minutes for a model with a dozen variables). For now, prune() and stepup() are the faster route to a sparse network.

Relation to lavaan

Models with covariance parameterizations (latent, residual and composite all "cov") are the models that lavaan 0.7 and later estimate with the <~ operator, and can be converted in both directions with fromlavaan() and tolavaan(): 

library("lavaan")

# psychonetrics -> lavaan:
lavfit <- tolavaan(mod2)
fitMeasures(lavfit, c("chisq", "df", "npar"))   # 3.01, 6, 21: as above

# lavaan -> psychonetrics:
fit <- sem(' Originals <~ Q5 + Q6 + Q7
             Sequels   =~ Q8 + Q9 + Q10
             Sequels   ~  Originals ',
           data = StarWars, meanstructure = TRUE, composites.cov = "free")
mod_from_lavaan <- fromlavaan(fit, run = TRUE)

One difference in defaults is worth knowing. lavaan by default fixes the variances and covariances of composite indicators to their sample values, whereas psychonetrics estimates them (composites.cov = "free" in lavaan). With complete data both give the same chi-square, degrees of freedom, weights, loadings and regressions with the same standard errors, but the number of parameters differs, and with it AIC and BIC. For the model above, the lavaan defaults count 9 parameters where psychonetrics counts 21: the difference is the six means of the observed variables and the six variances and covariances of Q5–Q7. Network parameterizations ("ggm", "prec") have no lavaan counterpart.

Experimental Status and Limitations

The family is new in version 0.18 and is marked experimental. Current limitations:

  • Ordinal data and correlation input are not supported.
  • latent can only be "cov" or "ggm".
  • With latent = "ggm", a composite cannot have both incoming effects and edges in the latent network.
  • With small samples or weak relations between constructs, the weights can be poorly determined: standard errors are then very large, and the optimizer can stop on a flat part of the likelihood. Check the warnings and the standard errors, estimate the model again with stricter convergence criteria, and consider another indicator to scale the composite.
  • The optimizer can stop slightly before the optimum in the weights. setoptimizer() gives stricter convergence (see Estimation).
  • Large models are slow: about 10 to 20 seconds for 36 variables, and minutes for 64 variables.
  • Penalized estimation can be slow (see above).

Summary

  • cvm() estimates structural equation models with latent variables (lambda) and composites (weights) in any combination
  • cnm() models the indicators within every composite as a partial correlation network (omega_c), which can be made sparse with prune() and stepup()
  • The _c matrices describe the indicators of composites, not the relations between composites
  • Variances and intercepts of composites are computed, not estimated
  • Models can be converted to and from lavaan with tolavaan() and fromlavaan()
  • clustersearch() searches for clusters of variables that form composites (or latent variables)
  • The family is experimental in version 0.18
